# What went wrong and what changed

## Environment and reward design

| Issue | What I changed | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|
| A large payout could end a short early campaign before work, rest or debt mattered. | Added opening hours and a shared casino cooldown, extended the campaign, and introduced explicit settlement before the garage. The resource environment later made both games negative in expected monetary return. | Early payout-driven wins were possible under those earlier rules. They show sensitivity to task design and realized luck. [Early environment reflection](../../baseline/reports/assignment1.md) and [game calculations](data/diagnostics/analytic_checks.json). |
| Cash, car debt and personal credit could be confused when interpreting quick wins. | Kept living cash and the two debt types separate. Each personal loan has its own ledger. New borrowing raises cash and liabilities equally before interest. Both debts must be zero before the garage completes ownership. | Loan creation and principal-only repayments cannot increase net worth by themselves. This was checked in the simulator, and the final follow-up verifies zero outstanding debt for every ownership outcome. [Environment](environment.md) and [accounting checks](data/diagnostics/environment_verification.json). |
| An early reward made delayed failure profitable relative to faster failure. | Rebalanced the continuing time cost and terminal failure penalty. For any finite failed episode the discounted **base** return is now −0.1. The declared wealth term remains separate. | The old incentive follows directly from the discount formula. The correction removes the base-reward benefit of postponing an inevitable failure. [Reward audit](../reward_audit.md). |
| A trip could make the agent appear inactive, or make a single food choice end before it could buy anything. | Used shared obstacle-aware navigation and extended a freely chosen service intention from 32 to up to 128 physical ticks. The agent still selects each service. Added requested-action and applied-control logs and a stopped-driving check. | All 60 tested early food trips could deliver a meal when the destination was kept active, while none completed within the old single 32-tick decision. Missed routes and crashes remain possible. [Food execution test](../../experiments/food_execution_diagnosis.json), [navigation calibration](data/diagnostics/navigation_h24_hold4.json) and [driver diagnostic method](../../experiments/driver_diagnostics.py). |
| The agent needed enough public information to compare meals. | Exposed locations, road distances, satiety, current prices, portion ranges and service time. An agent can choose any affordable food service; the environment still checks eligibility on arrival. | The later failed checkpoints already had the public menu. Missing menu inputs cannot explain those failures. [Public menu](../public_menu.md) and [trace diagnosis](../resource_study_review_2026-11-23/food_diagnosis/findings.md). |
| An automatic gambling action could look like a voluntary wager in the early prototype. | Logged the requested and applied action separately and disabled the automatic override for the historical voluntary-choice analysis. | The archived analysis can distinguish a policy-requested wager from an override. The current final follow-up separately counts wager intentions and started games. [Historical voluntary-gambling results](../../baseline/reports/debt_research.md#53-voluntary-continuation-and-loss-specific-response). |

## Learning and evaluation

| Issue | What I changed | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|
| PPO completed isolated food lessons, then starved in the campaign. | Replayed checkpoints from before and after the late lessons on identical food scenarios. Added a more gradual food-planning curriculum, varied cash and start locations, and required repeated retained-skill checks in a separate recovery protocol. | Both PPO variants still scored 16/16 in the identical food context at their final failed checkpoint. In the campaign they paid debt without keeping enough cash for food, or chose food too late. In the preserved standard games the two PPO models completed zero and one meals across 64 episodes each. The new safeguards passed engineering checks, while the short PPO stress test remained 0/16. [Decision evidence](data/diagnostics/decisions.csv) and [retention and transfer](transfer.md). |
| DSAC MLP could ignore food even when nearby and affordable. | Compared actor probabilities with critic scores. Tested forward-KL actor projection, categorical temperature, recent observation history and staged replay in separate continuations. | A saved critic favored the restaurant while the actor gave it probability about 8 × 10⁻¹⁰ in one probe. Forward KL recovered 16/16 at the restaurant, but it still won 0/16 standard episodes. The later stacked actor won 16/16 on an ownership-only reset yet lost all standard tests. The local fixes did not establish full-game learning. [Actor diagnostic](../local_food_recovery/ACTOR_DIAGNOSIS.md), [continuations](data/recovery_trials/) and [smoke tests](data/smoke/). |
| The learner could repeatedly request a closed or unaffordable operation. | Logged the intended action and whether it executed, and implemented a separate eight-tick refused-service treatment with full time and hunger accounting. | One DSAC MLP checkpoint made 3,904 one-tick failed work requests. A different checkpoint repeated borrowing and repayment. These loops consumed time and did not demonstrate an increase in wealth or rewarded progress. The longer feedback treatment passed the local interface checks. [Food and interface diagnosis](../resource_study_review_2026-11-23/food_diagnosis/findings.md) and [recovery verification](../resource_study_review_2026-11-23/recovery_status.md). |
| PPO exploration could create successful actions while the native actor assigned those actions almost no probability. | Measured both probabilities from saved observations and tested native actor sampling with a training-only exploration loss. | Early uniform exploration was accounted for in the likelihood ratio, but gave the native logits a weak gradient for excluded actions. The native-sampling update passed its GPU consistency check. It did not solve the full campaign in a short run. [PPO diagnosis](../local_food_recovery/PPO_DIAGNOSIS.md) and [training pipeline review](../local_food_recovery/PIPELINE_REVIEW.md). |
| Easy lesson gates and checkpoint selection could overstate readiness. | Made recovery require successful episodes that actually perform the relevant action, retest prior skills on two scenario banks, and export the same checkpoint that satisfies readiness. Kept historical runs and their frozen source. | A previous intermediate gate could advance at 4/16 ownership, and the selection path could export a different reward-ranked snapshot. These are weaknesses in the experimental protocol, independently of the environment objective. The recovery gate implementation was checked locally. [Recovery status](../resource_study_review_2026-11-23/recovery_status.md) and [checkpoint selection review](../local_food_recovery/PIPELINE_REVIEW.md). |
| The four-policy workflow gated final testing on all learners. | Kept the original gate and ran the qualified, frozen DSAC GRU alone on the registered 30-condition test suite. Replayed every condition from recorded decisions and verified its endpoints. | The single-policy run measures test performance of that checkpoint. The other three policies remain documented development failures. [Final evaluation](final_dsac_gru.md). |

## How to read starvation and reward hacking

In the current campaign, starvation is usually an endpoint of earlier choices. For example, on development scenario 1000001, PPO MLP paid down the car loan twice, each time leaving $0, then started another trip to work at low satiety. It did not choose food on that path. The earlier reward did favor later failure, but that is a different mechanism from the food-policy transfer failure measured here. Repeated failed requests, cash exhaustion, missed shifts and basic reward incentives should be diagnosed separately. [Original episode analysis](../resource_study_review_2026-11-23/food_diagnosis/findings.md) and [reward derivation](../reward_audit.md).

In the final DSAC GRU evaluation, the tested ownership and accounting checks found no confirmed reward-hacking episode. This is a scoped finding from replay, debt checks and traced transactions. It cannot certify every possible policy or reward component. The earlier delayed-failure incentive, short-horizon casino wins and unproductive service loops are recorded separately in the [results](results.md).
